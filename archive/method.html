Method · editorial governance

Labels, review, and correction made visible.

This docket explains how the archive decides what may be published, in what form, and under which conditions. It is the page that governs every other page.

Label legend

Six labels, one discipline.

A label describes the present editorial state of material. It does not rank lived knowledge, decide truth by itself, or replace careful reading of provenance, consent, language, and context.

L1

Verified evidence

Assessed within stated limits, with sources, provenance, and date of review recorded. Still open to correction.

L2

Documented record

A source exists and is described, but its interpretation may still be contested or incomplete.

L3

Community memory

Recollection or inherited knowledge, shared under agreed conditions. Not independently verified.

L4

Scholarly interpretation

An analytical reading situated in its methods, language, evidence base, and publication moment.

L5

Allegation

Attributed rather than adopted. Publication requires contextual and right-of-reply review.

L6

Unresolved / restricted

Sources conflict, context is missing, or access is withheld. Uncertainty is retained rather than filled in.

Review workflow

Five gates before publication.

Any gate can stop or narrow a description. A record that cannot pass is not published with a warning attached — it is held.

  1. Provenance

    Can we trace this material to a repository, contributor, or collection with stated conditions?

  2. Context

    Do we understand why it was made, for whom, and what it cannot establish?

  3. Language

    Are original terms preserved, translations described, and meaning shifts visible?

  4. Harm

    Could description, naming, or circulation expose or stigmatise anyone now?

  5. Consent and reply

    Have contributors and those named had a genuine opportunity to set conditions or respond?

Role A

Subject review

Checks provenance, framing, and the difference between record and interpretation.

Role B

Language review

Checks terminology, translation, register, and possible loss of meaning.

Role C

Safeguarding review

Assesses risk to living people, including identification through metadata.

Role D

Community review

Sits with people who hold the relationships, not only with researchers.

Glossary

Eight working terms, plainly defined.

Terminology in this archive is a working instrument, not a claim about the world. Definitions are revisable — and where a term is contested, that is stated.

Provenance
The history of a record’s creation, custody, and description — who made it, when, where it has been, and who has described it since.
Corroboration
Support from independently situated material. A second copy of the same claim is not corroboration.
Status label
A visible statement of a record’s present editorial condition. It is not a truth score.
Access condition
The terms under which material may be read, quoted, or circulated — including review-led, restricted, and private.
Minimisation
Collecting and publishing the least identifying detail required for the purpose.
Right of reply
A structured opportunity for a person, family, or community to respond to a contested account before or after publication.
Periodisation
How time is divided into periods. In this archive it is treated as an argument, not a fact.
Withdrawal
A contributor’s request that material no longer be published, quoted, or described. Honoured as a condition, not negotiated as a favour.
Limits of this framework

What the method cannot fix.

A method reduces harm; it does not eliminate it. Some material should never be published, some questions may never be answerable, and some disagreements are older and larger than this archive.

  • A label cannot make an unreliable source reliable
  • Consent cannot be obtained retroactively for everyone affected
  • Translation always loses something, and the loss is not always visible
  • An archive cannot adjudicate a land or political dispute
  • Absence from the record is not evidence of absence in history
