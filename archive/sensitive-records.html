Harm minimisation

Five rules that come before description.

Sensitive material is not a harder version of ordinary material. It requires a different order of decisions: minimise, protect, withhold, and only then consider describing.

01

Describe less

A public description may indicate that a protected item exists without exposing contents, source, identifying details, or the reason it is restricted.

02

Do not create discoverability

Wording, tags, and metadata can make material findable even when content is withheld. Searchability is a form of publication.

03

Preserve uncertainty

Where accounts conflict or context is missing, retain the uncertainty. Certainty is not a neutral default.

04

Right of reply first

Where a living person, family, or community is concerned, a response should be invited before publication or revision.

05

Record the decision

Note what was withheld, by whom, and why — so that the choice can be reviewed later without re-exposing anyone.

06

Make withdrawal possible

A clear takedown route should exist, and using it should not be treated as an admission of error.

Takedown and correction

Removal is a legitimate editorial outcome.

Where material is inaccurate, harmful, improperly attributed, or missing necessary context, the archive should provide a route for review. Corrections are not limited to factual error: they can address naming, translation, access, framing, consent, and whether a public record should remain available at all.

Request a correction Contribute with care
  1. Receive

    A concern, correction, or request for reply is received without assuming the existing description is complete or correct.

  2. Review context

    The wording, attribution, access conditions, provenance, and additional context are reviewed with appropriate care.

  3. Revise responsibly

    The archive may correct, contextualise, restrict, remove, or leave a record unresolved while making its editorial status visible.

  4. Record the outcome

    The decision and its reason are retained so that later readers can see that a change was made and why.
