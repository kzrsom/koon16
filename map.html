Consent and stewardship

Decisions about land require the people who live on it.

Decisions about land and development should be transparent, informed by affected communities, and attentive to water, livelihoods, environmental care, and future generations.

  • Consent is a condition, not a consultation step
  • Water access is treated as part of any land question
  • Development claims are read alongside livelihood impact
  • Women’s and minority tenure interests are named explicitly
  • Displacement is documented as harm, not as data
Land questions

What the archive asks about land.

These questions stay open because closing them requires evidence this platform does not hold — and because a wrong answer about land causes immediate harm.

Q1 — Tenure

How is access described across records and family knowledge?

Registers may record a category where a family records an arrangement. Comparing the two is research, not reconciliation.

Q2 — Water

Who controls irrigation, and how is that described?

Water rights are often entangled with land claims. Records of one rarely settle the other.

Q3 — Displacement

How is movement recorded, and who is not counted?

Administrative categories may omit people who never registered, or who left before registration existed.

Q4 — Naming

Which places appear under names that differ from local usage?

Naming is evidence of position. A catalogue term may reflect a record-maker’s framework rather than local self-description.
