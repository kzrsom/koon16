Reading key

Three kinds of entry, never merged.

Each item below carries a type. The type tells you what kind of support an entry has, and therefore how much it can carry in an argument.

Type A

Documented evidence

Materials with identifiable sources can still require context, interpretation, and corroboration.

Type B

Testimony

Community testimony carries knowledge, relationship, and obligations of consent that must be respected.

Type C

Interpretation and open questions

Interpretation should be labelled, and uncertainty should remain visible where evidence is incomplete.

Framework

Periods, records, and questions kept distinct.

Each entry identifies a possible research route rather than supplying an unverified event. Expand an entry to see the fields that would be needed before a public historical claim could be responsibly described.

Context 01 · Late nineteenth century

Regional change requires locally grounded reading

Research may examine changing political, commercial, religious, environmental, and social conditions across the wider region. This placeholder assigns no single cause, date, actor, or outcome to any community history.

Record 02 · Colonial-period documents

Administrative documents can preserve purpose as well as information

Catalogues, correspondence, reports, maps, and registers may be relevant to research, but they may also contain institutional vocabulary, omissions, and uneven points of view. Their existence alone does not verify the account they appear to offer.

View evidence fields
ProvenanceRepository, creator, date, collection history, and access conditions remain to be documented.
CorroborationNeeds contextual reading alongside other records and, where welcomed, community knowledge.
ConfidenceUnrated until an actual record and its context are reviewed.
Interpretation 03 · Wider Benadir resistance research

Resistance may be researched without reducing it to one form or story

Researchers may ask how refusal, negotiation, survival, mobility, labour, language, care, or public action are framed in different sources. This archive does not use the term as an automatic description of any particular event or person.

View evidence fields
Interpretive basisNo publication, author, or citation is supplied in this framework.
Analytical limitsInterpretation should remain separate from primary records and memory.
Verification statusInterpretive prompt only — not a verified conclusion.
Memory 04 · Intergenerational remembrance

Memory can guide research while retaining its own conditions of care

Community-held knowledge may preserve language, questions, relationships, and interpretive context. It should be described as memory unless separately supported and shared for that purpose under agreed conditions.

View evidence fields
Care conditionsNo testimony is included here; consent, attribution, and circulation preferences would be required first.
StatusCommunity memory — not independently verified.
PublicationDepends on an agreed access context and continued consent.
Context 05 · Twentieth-century institutions

Institutional records reveal procedures and their makers

Registers, minutes, and correspondence can show how categories were applied and who was counted. They rarely record how those categories were experienced.

Memory 06 · Contemporary stewardship

Living knowledge may be described by access rather than displayed

Where consent, safety, or community review is active, a record can exist publicly without its contents being reproduced.
