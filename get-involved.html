Before you send anything

Contributions are saved for review and are not automatically published. Do not submit sensitive material or anything that could put you or another person at risk.

Ways to help

Four useful routes, one of which is enough.

You do not need a complete record to contribute. You can share a source, flag a correction, suggest a research question, offer general support, or explain relevant context.

01

Story or memory

Describe what you know and how you came to know it. You decide what may be quoted and whether you are named.

02

Source or record

A document, photograph, map, or publication — plus where it came from and what limits it has.

03

Correction

Naming, translation, framing, access, and consent are all legitimate grounds for correction.

04

Support message

Time, translation, review, partnership, or a note of support. No obligation attached.

Safeguarding

What not to send.

  • Material that could identify a person who has not agreed to be identified
  • Anything about a minor, or about a living person in a contested account
  • Information about current disputes, threats, or ongoing risks
  • Scans or photographs of documents you do not have permission to share
  • Medical, legal, or financial details about yourself or others

If you are unsure, send a description only. The team can discuss conditions before anything is transmitted.

What happens next

Nothing is published automatically.

  1. Received

    Your contribution is logged with its stated conditions. You receive a reference number in a live system.

  2. Reviewed

    A reviewer checks context, provenance, consent, and risk. You may be asked for clarification.

  3. Decided

    The material may be described, held for community review, restricted, or declined — each with a reason.

  4. Recorded

    The decision, and any later correction, is retained so the record stays auditable.

Other ways to help

Roles that make the work possible.

The archive depends on people who translate, review, catalogue, and question. These are not volunteer “opportunities” so much as necessary functions.

01

Translation

Somali and English review of terminology, register, and meaning. Translation is treated as editorial work, not typing.

02

Review

Subject reviewers check provenance and framing. Community reviewers check consent, naming, and care.

03

Cataloguing

Describing material with its conditions: repository, creator, date range, language, access.

04

Questioning

Readers who disagree publicly and precisely improve the record more than readers who agree quietly.
